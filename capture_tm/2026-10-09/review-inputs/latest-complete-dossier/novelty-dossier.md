## Problem and candidate hypotheses

Frozen problem: under identical causal previews, legal physical actions, budget and fixed target, does exposure adaptation retain identifiable value after the renderer sufficiently uses available observations?

Candidate claims for examination, not asserted established:
C1. The remaining value of exposure choice depends on backend compensation/information scope.
C2. Matched pixel marginals with changed spatial dependence can alter useful capture choices identifiable from causal previews.
C3. Any resulting deployable benefit survives sufficient reconstruction and independent-backend evaluation and is not already explained by simple brightness/local rules.

The new diagnostic action catalog is [.125,.25,.5,1,2,4,8]. Toy latent marginals are a uniform (.05,.30) and b uniform .8..1.8, mixed independent/copula training prior. The fixed target is sqrt(l/(l+.5)). Three independent noisy previews at .25 are supplied to policies; future capture follows the declared Poisson/read/clip/ADC chain. The history-reference protocol contrasts renderers conditioned on future capture only or on all H plus future capture and independently freezes development selectors for a full crossover. N1 changes true dependence while the renderer's training prior stays fixed.

The DNG relative diagnostic is a separate protocol using bounded processed green content as a surrogate source, fixed target, identical relative action/noise assumptions, camera-specific train-only point priors and inverse/local-Wiener controls. Source provenance, background-group split and limitations are in raw audit/protocol files. This does not reproduce original S24 neural training or provide true multi-exposure recaptures.

## Required independent novelty assessment

Is the method or finding novel? What is the closest prior work? What is the actual delta?
Assess method and controlled experimental setting, including findings supported or unsupported by the complete raw evidence.
Check recent six months and foundational primary work. Distinguish a generic Bayes/denoising property from an original experimentally defensible finding.
Provide core-claim comparison, primary citations/sections, exact verifiable delta if any, calibrated novelty score and explicit PROCEED / PROCEED WITH CAUTION / ABANDON under the limits below.
If evidence is insufficient for a submission idea, say so and identify the cheapest discriminating next evidence. Do not treat a workflow receipt as manuscript readiness.

=== NOVELTY VERDICT LIMITS (these bound how you judge, never how widely you search) ===
Search exhaustively; judge calibrated. Two failures waste months equally:
passing an idea a published paper already contains, and killing a viable idea
because the territory has neighbors.
1. Proximity is information, not a verdict. Someone working nearby goes in the
   report; it is not by itself a reason to reject.
2. ABANDON has exactly one qualification: a specific published paper already
   contains this result — name that paper. No named paper, no ABANDON.
3. Crowded-but-deltaed is PROCEED: state the delta in one sentence a reviewer
   could verify. Thin or contested delta is PROCEED WITH CAUTION — say what
   would make it carry, not why it should die. CAUTION is not a safe middle:
   if you cannot name the specific thing that makes the delta thin, the
   verdict is PROCEED.
4. Concurrent or competing work is not a veto. That is a race — report it and
   let the user decide whether to run it.
5. A direct attack on a central problem is legitimate novelty when nobody has
   executed it well. "This area is hot" does not mean "this area is taken."
6. This check is an early gate, never the last one — more triage, pilots, or
   external review still stand between any idea and a paper, whatever order
   this run uses. A wrongly passed idea dies cheaply at one of them; a wrongly
   killed idea is never seen again. When torn between two verdicts, choose the
   more permissive one.
Say plainly when an idea clears the check. Do not manufacture overlap.
